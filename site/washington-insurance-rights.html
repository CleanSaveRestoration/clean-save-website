Water damage & insurance

Washington's new insurance rules put homeowners on a clock that works for you

Rules that took effect October 18, 2026 set firmer deadlines for how insurers handle property claims. If you've had water, fire or mold damage, here's what that means.

When water is coming through the ceiling, waiting on an insurance answer is the last thing you need. The Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner tightened the claim-handling rules (WAC 284-30). They set real deadlines for insurers and give you the right to see how your claim is being decided.

5
business days

For your insurer to approve the emergency mitigation plan or provide its own approved plan.

30
calendar days

To accept or deny your claim from when you report it, in most cases.

15
business days

To send you your claim file materials after you ask for them.

What the new rules give you

  • A fast answer on emergency drying. If your insurer cuts the mitigation plan, it has to say why and list the amounts it won't approve.
  • Pricing has to stand on more than software. An insurer can't rely only on a database or estimating program, and you can ask where its price and labor numbers came from.
  • A look inside your claim. You can request the adjuster's notes, estimates, photos and measurements. Anything withheld has to be disclosed.
  • Status updates when it drags on. If an investigation runs past 30 days, written updates have to list what's been decided and what's still open.
  • Fair treatment if you hire help. An insurer can't treat you differently for using a licensed public adjuster.
